2 Land & size
“Undeveloped” is about land; “vacant” is about a building. Undeveloped land simply has no structure and is not a sign of distress — it is the cleanest BESS land we hold, with nothing to demolish and no tenants to relieve. A vacant or abandoned building is an owner-motivation signal — section 4.

3 Power & grid
These measure distance to a wire, which is not the same as capacity to spare. A parcel beside a full substation is worse than one three miles from a bus with headroom. For capacity, use the Site screener.
Shown on the map
Substations, lines and buses are three different answers, and only one of them is about capacity. A line tells you power passes nearby; a substation tells you it is transformed nearby; a bus is the only one of the three that carries a number for how much you could actually take. A parcel beside a full substation is a worse site than one three miles from a bus with headroom — so use distance to decide construction cost, and the bus to decide whether the project is possible at all. See the Power & grid page for every bus in both directions.
The queue is your competition for the same study slots and the same headroom. Every point here is a project that got there first; a cluster of them around a bus is a warning that the number you are reading may already be spoken for by the time your study runs. ⚠ Candidate buses are ESTIMATED positions — no operator publishes a public coordinate feed for PJM buses, so these are matched from a substation gazetteer. Treat them as “about here”, never as surveyed.

The territory owning your parcel sets the tariff you pay, runs the load study, and determines whether the state commission is involved at all — an investor-owned utility answers to the IURC, a municipal or a REMC largely does not. ⚠ A parcel straddling two territories is reported as straddling rather than assigned to one, because guessing which one serves it would decide your rate for you.
Voltage class is the fastest read on the map — a 765 kV backbone and a sub-100 kV lateral look the same on a plain map and are not remotely the same siting fact. Unknown voltage has its own colour and is never drawn as low voltage. Gas matters if you intend on-site generation; a 300 MW plant needs roughly 50 MMcf/d. Large emitters signal neighbours who already hold air permits, so the permitting path is precedented rather than novel.
4 Owner motivation
Public records — unpaid taxes, a foreclosure, a demolition order — suggesting an owner may transact. Non-residential only, and only trouble serious enough to plausibly move someone to sell.
Undated is not old. 90% of our largest signal (unpaid taxes) carries no date, so a bare “last 90 days” cut would delete nine tenths of it for a reason about our collection, not about the property. They are kept by default; untick to exclude them.
5 Environmental constraints
Keep only sites clear of…
⚠ We highlight the PARCELS, not the hazard polygons. The flood and wetland source layers are 804 MB and 1.3 GB — far too large to send to a browser — so what we ship is a per-parcel flag measured against them. That answers “is this site affected”, which is the siting question; it will not draw you the floodplain itself.
The first three are hard constraints that can stop a project. The last is an upside — areas where a coal plant or mine closed carry a larger federal tax credit.
Shown on the map
Five of these six are money and the sixth can stop the project. An energy community or low-income designation adds up to 10 percentage points to the investment tax credit, which on a capital-heavy build is the largest single swing on this whole console — worth chasing deliberately rather than discovering later. Critical habitat is the opposite: a federal consultation that can add a year or end the siting outright. They were one checkbox until 2026-08-19, which meant a developer chasing the adder could not separate it from the blocker.
These four say who else already holds a say over the land, which is not something zoning or the environmental gates will tell you. A large load near an active installation can draw a DoD Siting Clearinghouse review that runs on the Department’s clock, not the county’s — it rarely refuses, it adds months. Tribal trust land is a different sovereign entirely, so the counterparty is the tribal government rather than the county. Special-use airspace governs what may be built tall — cooling towers, stacks and cranes, not the slab. And every obstruction drawn here is at or above the 200 ft FAA notice threshold, so a cluster of them is cheap evidence that a tall structure of your own will clear the same review.
A watershed is the unit water is actually allocated and argued over in — two parcels a mile apart in different subbasins can face different objections and different permitting bodies. Click one to see the rivers, reservoirs and lakes it holds. Power generation is already Indiana’s largest water user (3,822 of 7,177 Mgal/d) and 99.5% of that is surface water, so a new thermal load competes for exactly what these layers show.

Saves every screener setting, your scoring weights, which layers are on and the current map view, under a name. Stored in this browser only — nothing is uploaded, and it will not follow you to another machine.

Score your own sites (first-class, same pipeline)

Excel (.xlsx) or CSV — any layout. We find the header row even if it is not the first, then ask you which columns are which rather than guessing silently. Read in your browser; nothing uploads anywhere, because there is nowhere to upload to. Each site gets grid distances, county gates & sentiment and screener context. Rows without coordinates are kept and listed, never dropped.
