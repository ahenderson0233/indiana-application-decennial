## ⭐ 3. PJM PARITY — THE VENDOR'S RULE WAS IN A COLUMN OF THEIR FILE WE HAD NEVER READ

Their capacity export carries **`Shift Factor Cutoff Ratio`**, and it is **not one number**:

| | cutoff | rows |
|---|---|---:|
| `Existing Overload Flag = false` | **0.05** | 279 |
| `Existing Overload Flag = true` | **0.20** | 18 |

A facility already over its rating only binds if the bus **strongly** drives it — and those 18 rows
are exactly the 6.1% of buses the vendor reports at **zero**.

We applied a flat 0.05 and ordered by `facility_overloaded ASC`, which skipped an over-rating
facility for the next healthy one. **So we reported a positive number for every bus in the state and
never once said zero.** ⚠ `mw_clean` had the same bug and had to move with the `ORDER BY`, or the row
would *name* an overloaded binder while *reporting* a healthy facility's capacity.

**Like-for-like, 282 shared Indiana tier-0 PJM withdrawal buses:**

| | >0 | median | mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vendor | 96.8% | 227.5 | 332.6 |
| **ours now** | **95.7%** | 276.7 | 436.6 |
| ours before | 100.0% | 284.5 | 460.8 |

median ratio **1.056** · **"they say 0 and we don't": 18 → 0**

⭐ **The arithmetic was never the problem.** Comparing binding facilities per bus, they agree on
**100 of 282**, and **where they agree the ratio is median 1.001 with 68 of 91 inside 5%.**

⛔ **THE REAL CEILING, measured:** the vendor's binding facility is **absent from our harvest on 117
of 282 buses (41.5%)**. No rule change reaches that.

⚠ **Two instrument errors I made here, both worth not repeating.** I first compared our 1,826-bus
**AEP-footprint** distribution against their 297-bus **Indiana** slice and called it a gap — the
parity doc had flagged that exact trap. And I reported "binding facility agrees on 0 of 282" when
their strings are space-padded and I had matched them raw; normalised, it is 100.

## ⛔ 9. TRAPS — THE 2026-08-19 SET

1. **A `SHORT` line in the harvest log is NOT data loss.** Use `audit_pjm_short_reads.py`, never the
   log. Stopping a healthy harvest over one created the batch-30 gap.
2. **Killing the ladder supervisor kills its python child seconds later**, even though an immediate
   check says otherwise.
3. **`nulls` and `rows` are BigQuery reserved words**, and a swallowing `except` turns that into
   "every table MISSING".
4. **Never guess a column name OR A VALUE VOCABULARY.** This session: `value` (it is `rate`),
   and G94's `investor_owned` vs `INVESTOR OWNED`, which silently greyed out all 145 territories.
5. **Never write a regex through a shell heredoc.** Broken again this session; the escapes mangle.
   Use the Write tool and self-test at import.
6. ⭐ **A dry run that writes is worse than no dry run.** `apply_action_verification.py` ran its
   UPDATE unconditionally and gated only the registry insert on `--apply`.
7. ⭐ **A re-check may CONFIRM or ADVANCE a row — never silently DEMOTE one.** Marking Marion
   NOT_FOUND because a later milestone had not happened knocked a verified action off the map.
8. ⭐ **Sampling one row and generalising.** I read one county's context block and concluded the
   actions data was missing; it ships on 40.
9. **`Number(null)` is 0, and 0 is finite.** A coordinate-less parcel measured from the Gulf of
   Guinea and reported a military base "5,966 mi" away.
10. ⚠ **`fetchGz()` sends no cache-buster** (G101). After a payload rebuild the browser serves the
    old file, which **masquerades as a build failure**.

**The pattern behind all of them: a clean, alarming or UNCHANGED number is a claim about your
INSTRUMENT first.** Three audits cried wolf or stayed silent this session — G76's criterion, the
G8 codename probe, and the superseded-table check (**G108**).
