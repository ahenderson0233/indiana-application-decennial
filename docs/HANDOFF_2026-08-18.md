## ⚠ 4. THE TARIFF WORK IS **NOT** FINISHED

**Done:** the five IOUs are costed and audited against EIA-861 **industrial** realized averages
(operator's call — industrial, not C&I, because the customer is large load). At 300 MW / 85% LF:
**26 priced rows, 15 within ±20%, 11 outside, 2 refused as not costable, 4 excluded on a real
eligibility ceiling.** I&M **IP-LL** is the best behaved — 7.06 / 7.45 / 8.56 / 9.54¢ across four
classes, all riders and the embedded capacity charge itemised.

⛔ **NOT done — and this is the largest open item (G55):** the **252 municipal rows have never been
through the costing audit.** Every defect below was found by running the calculator over the IOUs
and reconciling; the municipals have had **no equivalent pass**, they are **19 publishers with 19
sets of conventions**, and **most have no EIA-861 industrial benchmark at all**, so the gate that
caught the IOU errors does not exist for them.

### The nine defects, because the pattern is the lesson

Each was a **generic rule meeting a house convention**:

1. substring rider matching — `HL` caught `HL1/HL2/HL3`, one rider counted three times
2. `"HL (all voltages), PL"` read as a blanket — HL riders leaked onto SL and PH
3. block ladders **summed** rather than allocated — NIPSCO at **57.94¢/kWh**
4. `fuel_base` added as a charge when it is already **embedded** in the energy rate
5. demand billed flat rather than **seasonally**
6. "best voltage" chosen by lowest cost — which picked whichever class had the **least data**
7. AES's *low-load-factor transmission* summed with plain *transmission* → **20.64¢, dearer than
   secondary**, which was the tell
8. NIPSCO's `transmission/subtransmission` **slash** unrecognised → a $35.74/kW-month demand charge
   bound to sub-transmission alone, transmission row showed **DEMAND $0**
9. I&M writes **"Tariff I.P."** with periods → tokenised to `I` and `P`, never `IP`, so **all eight
   I&M riders (~+8.6 $/kW-month) silently failed to attach**

⭐ **The guard that matters more than any individual fix: a row missing a whole billing leg now
refuses to show an effective rate.** Every one of those bugs shared that signature.

⭐ **G56 — DONE. The parser is now per-utility adapters** (`scripts/tariff_adapters.py`), which was
the operator's structural call and the right one: several fixes for one utility had broken another.
Each IOU declares its own conventions — I&M's `I.P.` punctuation, AES's low-load-factor class,
Duke's split kV bands and floor-worded-as-ceiling row, NIPSCO's slash separator and 25 MW ceilings,
SIGECO's kVA billing — with the shared arithmetic left in `export_tariffs.py`.

**Verified as a refactor rather than a behaviour change: 15 of 18 IOU schedules are structurally
identical before and after**, and the three that moved GAINED classes the generic matcher had
collapsed. It paid for itself immediately — NIPSCO 631's classes had degraded to *"all tiers"* and
*"aggregated premises with BTM generation"*, and one line of `explicit_classes` in NIPSCO's own
section fixed it without touching any other publisher.

⚠ **The 19 municipals have NO adapter** and fall back to the generic path — the same path that
produced nine defects on five utilities. That is the first half of G55.
