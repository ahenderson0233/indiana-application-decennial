Gas OAC — Indiana locations (Panhandle Eastern & Trunkline publish county)

So what: this is the only gas number at a grain you can site against — a named delivery point, in a named county, with what the pipeline says is unsold today. The last column turns that into the generation it could actually support. Roughly 50 MMcf/d feeds a 300 MW plant, so a point posting a few thousand dekatherms a day is a boiler connection, not a data-centre power supply.
What to do with it: shortlist points whose spare capacity clears your load with margin, then confirm with the pipeline — posted capacity is a daily operational figure, not a reservation, and it can be sold to someone else tomorrow. Texas Eastern and REX are BLOCKED (walls recorded), so a gap here is not an absence of gas.

Delivered fuel cost at Indiana plants ($/MMBtu)

The ¢/kWh column converts it at a combined-cycle heat rate so you can hold it against a retail tariff: a bundled quote sitting near this number is a modelling error, not a bargain. Demand response — load the utilities can already shed

So what: two readings. It measures how much peak the utility can already remove without building anything, so a utility with deep demand response has more room for a new large load than its headline capacity suggests. It also signals whether an interruptible or curtailable tariff is likely to be offered to you — those trade a lower price for the utility's right to cut you, which suits a BESS far better than a data centre carrying an uptime commitment. Context for the negotiation, not a site test.
