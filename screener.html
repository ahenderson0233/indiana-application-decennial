What this page does

Tell it what you want to build. It ranks Indiana parcels that could host it, and shows how much electricity each one can actually get — not just how close the nearest power line happens to be.

Nothing here is a recommendation. Every number names where it came from, and anything we could not measure says so instead of showing a zero.

1 What are you building?

Megawatts (MW) of electrical demand. A large data centre campus is roughly 300–1,000 MW; a battery site here is 5 MW or less.

How much power you assume you can fit on an acre. 4 is a common planning figure for a data centre, 10 for batteries. This is your assumption to change — it is not something we measured.

2 Land & size

These describe what is on the land today, not what it could become. A farmhouse on 200 acres is classed residential and is still exactly the kind of site a developer wants, so we keep it.

“Undeveloped” is about land. “Vacant” is about a building.

Undeveloped land has no structure on it — that is simply what the parcel is, and it is not a sign of distress. A vacant or abandoned building is a structure standing empty, which is an owner-motivation signal (step 4). We keep these words apart because they mean completely different things to a buyer.

3 Power & grid
Two different questions, and they are not interchangeable.
Can this site GET power? What a data centre needs. The grid calls this withdrawal.
Can this site SEND power? What a power plant or a solar farm needs. The grid calls this injection.

A connection point can be wide open in one direction and full in the other. A big number in the wrong direction tells you nothing about your project, so we never merge them into one figure.

We only looked within 25 miles. Past that, a connection point is not a useful fact about the site.

Substation (a different thing again)

Distance to a substation says a wire is nearby. It does not say there is capacity to spare. 0 turns this filter off.

4 Owner motivation

Public records that suggest an owner may be open to selling — unpaid taxes, a foreclosure filing, a demolition order, a business dissolving. We only count non-residential property, and only trouble serious enough that it would plausibly move someone to sell. A parking ticket does not qualify.

How recent does it have to be?
Some signals go stale. Others never do.
One-off events A foreclosure, a fire, a violation. These have a date and they age — a 2014 filing says little about today's owner. The cutoff applies to these.
Standing conditions Contamination, an abandoned building, a delisted plant. These do not expire, so the cutoff never removes them.
No date recorded We hold the record but not when it happened — true for about a third of everything, and for 90% of unpaid-tax records. Your choice below.

Unticking this hides a large amount of real inventory for a reason that is about our data, not about the property.

5 Environmental constraints

A flood zone here means FEMA's Special Flood Hazard Area — the area with a 1-in-100 chance of flooding in any year. It is a hard constraint, not a preference.

Areas the federal government designates because a coal plant or mine closed there — projects built in them can claim a larger tax credit.
